Lessons 3-5 Sources

This note collects primary sources for the third, fourth, and fifth lessons. It complements 0001-market-signal-map-sources.md.

Lesson 3: Python and pandas financial data workflow

  • pandas: read/write tabular data Use for reading CSV data with read_csv, then checking head, dtypes, and info before calculating anything.
  • pandas: time series/date functionality Use for DatetimeIndex, date parsing, resampling, time zones, and business-day thinking.
  • pandas: DataFrame.pct_change Use for turning price into fractional return. The course should explicitly say this is fractional change, not already multiplied by 100.
  • pandas: windowing operations Use for rolling windows, expanding windows, exponentially weighted windows, moving averages, and rolling volatility.
  • pandas: DataFrame.shift Use for the first anti-lookahead rule: a signal known after today's close should be shifted before it is applied to next-period returns.
  • NumPy: the absolute basics Use to explain vectorized array thinking to a learner with C++/Java background.

Lesson 4: Minimal backtest mechanics and bias controls

  • QuantConnect: Time Modeling - Periods Use for bar timing: a period has start and end time, and the close is only known after the period ends.
  • QuantConnect: Timeslices Use to contrast vectorized toy backtests with event-driven backtests where the algorithm only receives current and past slices.
  • QuantConnect: Reality Modeling Use as the checklist for fills, slippage, fees, brokerage, buying power, settlement, short availability, margin, and capacity.
  • QuantConnect: Research Guide Use for look-ahead bias, survivorship bias, overfitting, outliers, and paper-trading discipline.
  • scikit-learn: TimeSeriesSplit Use for time-ordered validation. The important lesson is "past trains, future tests"; do not randomly shuffle financial time series.
  • Bailey et al.: The Probability of Backtest Overfitting Use to warn that many strategy trials can manufacture an attractive backtest.
  • Bailey and Lopez de Prado: The Deflated Sharpe Ratio Use to explain that Sharpe ratio can be inflated by selection bias, multiple testing, and non-normal returns.

Lesson 5: Futures basics and commodity bridge

  • CME: Definition of a Futures Contract Use for standardized contract, delivery month, exchange trading, central clearing, and price discovery.
  • CME: Margin - Know What's Needed Use to explain that futures margin is not a stock down payment. It is capital deposited and kept with the broker to hold a futures position, and margin calls can force liquidation.
  • CFTC: Commitments of Traders Use for COT reports, trader categories, open interest, reportable positions, and historical positioning data.
  • QuantConnect: Futures Universes and Continuous Contracts Use to explain continuous futures series, mapped tradable contracts, and why adjusted continuous prices are not directly tradable.
  • QuantConnect: Futures Handling Data Use for rollovers, contract changes, and event handling around mapped contracts.
  • LME Copper Use only as a bridge example at the end of lesson 5, so the course does not become overly specific too early.
  • CME Copper Futures Use to show that the same commodity can have different exchange contracts and specifications.
